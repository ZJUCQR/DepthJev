DepthJev

Turning depth into text for embodied navigation

The decision model never sees a pixel. Every frame becomes metric depth and a target box, the geometry becomes a few lines of text in metres, and Jev picks the next move from that text alone. It succeeds in 46.7% of all 300 EB-Navigation episodes, more often than Claude-3.5-Sonnet looking at the image.

Method

From a frame to a paragraph

Each step runs through three stages, and each stage is one sub-package of the code. Perception measures the room in metres, the language layer writes the measurements down, and Jev, a decision model that answers typed questions with probabilities, picks one of eight actions.

  1. 1Perceiveperception/

    Depth Anything 3 turns the 500 × 500 frame into metric depth and OWLv2 finds the target. The depth is back-projected, levelled against the floor and cut into five sectors.

    DA3 · 0.15 sOWLv2 · 0.22 s

  2. 2Describelanguage/

    Free space per sector, a collision check for the next 0.25 m step, the target's sector and distance, and the last three actions, written in four distance bins.

    under 0.5 m0.5 to 1 m1 to 2 mover 2 m

  3. 3Actdecision/

    Jev answers typed questions with probabilities: the target's object type once per episode, where the target is while it is out of view, and the next of eight actions.

    Jev · 0.34 s8 actions

    Latency

    Fast enough for a robot

    Mean server time per step on one H100. The three models run one after the other, and Jev's answer is a network request. Three runs sharing one GPU still finish a step in about a second.

    06

    Limits

    Where it fails

    160 episodes end without reaching the target. The largest group starts in perception: the target is rarely detected, or another object is taken for it.

    • Detection. The largest group includes 17 visual_appearance episodes where Jev picked the wrong object type from a description of looks. Adding “trash can” and “bin” as detector queries took GarbageCan from 0/5 to 5/5 on a development sample.
    • Action constraints. Writing a constraint into the action option itself (“NOT USEFUL NOW: the camera already looks 30 degrees down”) stopped repeated look_down better than a general rule.
    • Scene difficulty. Across the five instruction styles, the same 12 scenes always succeed and the same 22 always fail. long_horizon also turns the start pose by 180°.
